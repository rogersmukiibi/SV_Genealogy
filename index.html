    About this map

    Every box is a company; rounded pills are people who acted on their own. Lines show how each descends from earlier ones: founders who left to start it, alumni who joined, money that backed it, or assets it was built from. Traces end at a handful of roots, above all Shockley Semiconductor Laboratory (1956) and the eight engineers who left it to found Fairchild.

    The map runs from the transistor to Web 2.0, 1956–2007. Use the slider or ▶ to watch the Valley grow year by year. Click any company to see its path back to Shockley.

      Sources

      • The semiconductor backbone up to 1981 follows SEMI's 1984 “Silicon Valley Genealogy” poster, developed by Don C. Hoefler from a concept by Jack Yelverton. The poster inspired this map; the map is not a copy of it.
      • Everything else, including venture funding, is checked against the sources listed on each company and link: mostly Wikipedia, the Computer History Museum, company histories and filings. Where sources disagree, the company's note says so.
